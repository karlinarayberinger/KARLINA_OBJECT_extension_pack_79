### 10. [karbytes_content_management_policy](https://karbytesforlifeblog.wordpress.com/karbytes_content_management_policy/)

**Description.** The last target is a compact policy page last updated 18 October 2025. PART_0 points to the defining page for the collection, the macro-directory intended to encompass its micro-directories, and the separate definition of a micro-directory. PART_1 sets out an archival status convention dated to 17 October 2025 at 11:59 PM Pacific Standard Time. Under that convention, a repository whose ZIP filename in the current M_DISC catalog has no date stamp is considered an immutable historical snapshot; a repository whose ZIP filename contains a date component remains open to modifications until that component is removed. The page gives examples of stamped and unstamped names and locates the stamp between “-main” and “.zip.”

It also says karbytes uses a dedicated Python script to generate or regenerate checksums for repository ZIP files and stores checksum records in current and future repositories. It repeats that only Kar Beringer is authorized to modify the designated collection and that more than ten consecutive years without authorized changes establishes, by karbytes’s decree, a permanent immutable configuration. The page declares itself public domain. ([KARBYTES_FOR_LIFE_BLOG][8])

**My commentary.** I find the filename convention an unusually direct way to make an archive’s status visible. A reader who knows the convention can look at a catalog entry and see whether karbytes intended that repository to be still open or to stand as a frozen snapshot. The checksum records serve a different purpose: they offer a way to compare bytes, whereas a filename communicates the author’s declared stage in the workflow. Neither alone tells the whole history of a later correction.

This final page returns me to the small event that prompted the journal. A collection can have a policy of freezing older components and still require a limited correction when a visible link disagrees with its destination. The 23 September reopening of extension pack 78, and karbytes’s subsequent statement that its generated checksums, size approximations, and M_DISC ZIP catalog were not refreshed then, should be kept distinct from this 2025 description of the standing archival method. I read that distinction as part of the record’s value: it lets an intended rule, a dated catalog, and an actual later edit remain separately identifiable.
