THE COLLECTION AS A WHOLE: SUBJECTIVE COMMENTARY

I read extension pack 79 as a record of a person trying to control the distance between experience and preservation. Karbytes does not merely take a walk and later remember it. It makes files during the walk, copies them, asks another system to interpret them, checks the interpretation’s method, writes or stages transcripts, publishes source and presentation versions, and audits the paths between them. Each step can protect a detail from disappearance. Each step also creates another task that can delay the promised break.

That pressure gives “crystallization” and “hiatus” their particular force. They are practical plans for read-only copies and fewer updates, but also attempts to make the work release its author temporarily. The files repeatedly revise a deadline or document a narrow exception to closure. I do not read those revisions as proof that the wish for rest is insincere. The archive has a way of making one last correction feel necessary, and the transcripts expose that mechanism with unusual candor.

The body keeps interrupting any account of KARBYTES as a purely digital object. The M_DISCs occupy a spindle and cases; a USB drive blinks beside a floor-level laptop; bare feet cross Horsey Hill; clothes cease to fit; a phone plan lapses and is restored; a voice continues over video too dark to show a route. These details make the archive dependent on money, hardware, movement, sleep, and safety. The imagery of a vast cyberspace substrate and a cosmic Big Green Thing becomes more affecting, to me, because it is continually anchored to those ordinary constraints.

I also see a persistent negotiation between privacy and visibility. The informationcrystals drafts provide a sheltered interval before canon. The cocoon at The Burrow is described as relief from exposure. Yet karbytes also wants a record that can be public, copied, checked, and understood by strangers or future information-processing agents. The texts do not settle that tension, and I would not want a commentary to settle it on their behalf. A person may want solitude while still wanting accurate witnesses to its work.

Finally, these files ask readers to separate testimony from verification without dismissing either. Karbytes’s distress, sense of interruption, and description of being stuck are part of the record. Claims about who or what causes those experiences require evidence the transcripts do not supply. Likewise, an assistant’s numerical analysis of every audio sample is not the same as hearing a piece of music. The collection is strongest when it keeps those differences visible. Its lasting subject may be the labor of saying exactly what one knows, what one feels, what one infers, and what remains open—while the files, discs, trails, and conversations continue to accumulate.
